# Marketing Experiment — Business Recommendation

## Executive Summary

The advertising campaign produced a statistically significant increase in conversion compared with the PSA control.

- **Advertising conversion rate:** 2.555%
- **PSA conversion rate:** 1.785%
- **Absolute lift:** +0.769 percentage points
- **Relative lift:** +43.1%
- **95% CI for absolute lift:** +0.595 to +0.943 percentage points
- **Estimated incremental conversions:** ~4,343
- **95% CI for incremental conversions:** ~3,360 to ~5,326

The experiment provides strong evidence that assignment to advertising increased conversions relative to the PSA condition.

## Business Recommendation

Continue the advertising campaign **if the value generated by incremental conversions exceeds campaign cost**.

Because the dataset does not provide advertising spend or contribution value per conversion, profitability cannot be calculated directly. Instead, the experiment provides break-even thresholds that can be compared with actual campaign economics.

For example, if each incremental conversion contributes **$100 in business value**, the estimated 4,343 incremental conversions imply a break-even campaign cost of approximately **$434,000**.

Using the lower bound of the treatment-effect confidence interval provides a more conservative break-even threshold of approximately **$336,000**.

If actual campaign costs fall comfortably below this threshold, continued investment would be supported by the experimental evidence.

## Exposure Frequency

Conversion increased substantially with observed exposure frequency, but this pattern appeared in both the advertising and PSA groups.

This suggests that exposure frequency is also associated with underlying user engagement or opportunity to convert. Because exposure frequency was not independently randomized, the analysis does not establish that showing users more ads causes higher conversion.

A separate randomized frequency experiment would be required before recommending an optimal frequency cap.

## Campaign Timing

Conversion patterns varied across both day of week and hour of day. However, these timing variables were not independently randomized and some PSA time segments contained relatively small samples.

The observed timing patterns should therefore be treated as hypotheses for future experiments rather than evidence for immediately changing campaign scheduling.

## Recommended Next Steps

1. Compare actual campaign spend with the break-even thresholds from the experiment.
2. Incorporate contribution margin or customer value into future experiment reporting.
3. Run a randomized ad-frequency experiment to estimate diminishing returns and identify an efficient frequency cap.
4. Test campaign timing through randomized delivery windows before implementing timing-based targeting.
